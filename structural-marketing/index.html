Structural Marketing

The 9 Pillar Operating System

Most marketing problems are not creative problems. They are structural ones: a position, a price or a channel that was never designed to carry the load put on it.

Nine pillars, one system

Structural Marketing: The 9 Pillar Operating System rests on nine pillars. Each answers one question, in order, and every answer is checked against the others, and checks every answer against the others. A weak answer anywhere shows up as a cost somewhere else.

  1. 01

    COMPETE

    Where can we win, and against whom?

  2. 02

    WHY

    What are we choosing, and what are we refusing?

  3. 03

    WHO

    Which customers create value worth serving?

  4. 04

    WHAT

    What do we promise, and how do we prove it?

  5. 05

    HOW MUCH

    How do we price and capture the value we create?

  6. 06

    WHERE

    Where do customers find, buy and use it?

  7. 07

    WHEN

    When do we move, and in what order?

  8. 08

    HOW

    How does the organisation actually execute?

  9. 09

    SO WHAT

    What did it produce, in the language of the P&L?

The tenth volume, THE SYSTEMS, shows how the nine fit together.

A note on the name

The phrase “structural marketing” is also used in academic research, for a different idea. Ju-Yeon Lee, Irina V. Kozlenkova and Robert W. Palmatier introduced it in “Structural marketing: using organizational structure to achieve marketing objectives” (Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 43, 73–99, 2015). There it means using a firm’s organisational structure (how units are designed, who reports to whom, and how work is coordinated) as a marketing tool.

Structural Marketing: The 9 Pillar Operating System, on this site and in the ten volumes by Amrutha Reddy Konala, is a separate body of work: a method built on nine pillars that answers nine questions, from competition to measurement, as one system. The two share a name, not a definition.
